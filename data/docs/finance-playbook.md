# Personal Finance Playbook

## The 50/30/20 Rule

A simple starting budget splits after-tax income into three buckets: 50 percent for needs (rent, groceries, bills), 30 percent for wants (dining out, hobbies), and 20 percent for savings and debt repayment. If your housing costs alone exceed 50 percent, the rule still works as a diagnostic: it tells you the biggest lever is reducing fixed costs or increasing income.

## Emergency Fund

Before investing aggressively, build an emergency fund of three to six months of essential expenses in a high-interest savings account. This fund exists so a car repair or a job gap does not force you into high-interest debt. Replenish it immediately after any withdrawal.

## Debt Payoff

There are two popular strategies. The avalanche method pays the highest-interest debt first and minimizes total interest paid. The snowball method pays the smallest balance first and builds psychological momentum. Both work; the best one is the one you will stick with. Avoid carrying a credit card balance month to month, since typical APRs above 19 percent erase investment gains.

## Investing Basics

Start with tax-advantaged accounts before taxable ones. Diversify with low-cost index funds rather than picking individual stocks. Contribute consistently; time in the market beats timing the market. A common guideline is to invest at least 15 percent of gross income for retirement once high-interest debt is gone.

## Tracking

Review your spending once a month. Cancel subscriptions you do not use. Automate transfers to savings on payday so you never rely on willpower alone. Net worth equals assets minus liabilities; track it quarterly to see the long-term trend.
